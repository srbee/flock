How the Boids algorithm works

Each bird follows three simple local rules. There is no leader and no central controller.

  1. Separation — keep a safe distance from nearby birds to avoid collisions.
  2. Alignment — steer in roughly the same direction and speed as nearby birds.
  3. Cohesion — move towards the average position of nearby birds to stay with the flock.

Three simple rules → coordinated flock behaviour. This is called emergence.

The Boids model was introduced by computer scientist Craig Reynolds in 1986.
